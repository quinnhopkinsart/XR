# Notes for Claude

- Each XR work is one folder in `src/projects/<slug>/` with a `project.js` default export. The registry auto-discovers it. Do not add works to a central list.
- Field rules live in `src/projects/schema.js`; the human guide is `docs/ADDING-A-PROJECT.md`. Keep the two in sync when a field changes.
- Scaffold new works with `npm run new -- "Title"` (add `--build` for a self-hosted 8th Wall build).
- Colours and type come only from `src/brand/tokens.css`. Components never hard-code colours.
- Copy follows Quinn's voice: first person for site copy, declarative present tense for work descriptions, no em-dashes, no rhetorical questions, no "immersive", "stunning", "vibrant", "unique", "bridging two worlds". Anishinaabemowin is translated in the same clause on first use, never italicized. Non-human figures are relatives, presences and forces, never symbols.
- Run `npm run build` before committing; it runs `npm run check` first.
- 8th.io links stop working on February 28, 2027. Use `legacy` on any work still pointing at one.
- The floating background (`src/field/`) collects `models` from every project.js. Add 3D pieces there, never in the field code. The field is curated by Quinn, not every work with models shows them: right now only Trading Post Humanism and 13 Moon Calendar do, and Stellar Narratives and Wildlife Portals XR keep their .glb files with `models` commented out. Ask before adding a work's pieces to the field. Each model is one sculpture: a `split` model keeps its parts roughly where the original has them, each a little out of place, like a ruin (`turn` in degrees faces it to the page); an unsplit model floats whole and tumbles. Sculptures sit at mixed depths around the focal plane (z = 0), and on desktop a lens pass (three's BokehPass) blurs what is off it, so the canvas paints the ground colour itself there; phones skip the lens. Keep each .glb under about 2 MB (Draco via `npx @gltf-transform/cli draco`).
- Palette: orange-red `--ground`, `--black` for text and panels, `--saffron` as the one accent, `--oxblood` in shadows, `--white` in tiny touches only. Text on `--ground` is black; text on black panels is `--on-panel` (orange-red) or `--on-panel-strong` (white) for titles; text on saffron is black. Never saffron or white text on the ground. Check contrast before adding a new pairing.
- Nova Round (`--font-wordmark`, self-hosted in src/brand/fonts, SIL OFL) sets the header wordmark and the landing title. The landing title is WideTitle.jsx: one line at Nova Round's natural proportions, fitted to the content column like the portfolio's wordmark (`fitTitle` in its js/ui.js; keep the two fits the same), made bold with a text stroke since Nova Round has one weight. XR is a white badge in front of NOODIN's bottom-right corner (`badge` prop, `.wide-title__badge`) with its own parallax from pointer, tilt and scroll; white is allowed here only because it is large text (3.7:1, 28px floor). Everything else keeps Sora and Inter. The letters people see are drawn on a canvas over that text by `src/components/titleSignal.js` (the DOM text stays for layout, the fit and as the fallback): a light in the `--title-light-*` colours moves through the core of the letters with the pointer or phone tilt, a white scan line follows it as a running sine wave (animated only while the pointer is near the title), the rows under the line shift sideways a little with faint colour fringes while the pointer is moving (the `GLITCH` values in titleSignal.js; Quinn wants this subtle), and four corner marks close in like a tracked image target. It holds still when left alone, when Pause is on, and for reduced-motion visitors, who get one lit frame. A black rim stays on every letter so the title keeps its contrast on the red. While the title is on screen the floating field drops to 55% (`html.title-leads`).
- Text that sits straight on the red ground gets a solid red plate (`<span class="plate">`, or the plate rules for short labels in global.css), so floating pieces pass behind the plate, never behind the letters.
- The Trading Post Humanism build has two additions outside the 8th Wall export: the logo link (`#brand-home`, `/` with target="_top") and tilt-to-look (`tilt.js`, loaded after bundle.js, plus the `#tilt-btn` button, its CSS and the "Tilt or drag" hint in index.html). tilt.js turns phone tilt into the pointermove events the 3D view already reads. Re-exporting overwrites index.html, so carry these into the source project or re-add them.
- Biidaaban's build (`public/builds/biidaaban/`) has no additions outside its source: the 3D carousel, Play button, captions and tilt are in the TwoEyedSeeing 8th Wall project's `src/desktop/` and `src/common/`. Rebuild there and copy `dist/` across, see public/builds/README.md. Its 3D view clips each panel to its painting instead of using the AR hiders, so panels can sit side by side.
- `award` in project.js puts an award logo on the home card (black tab, top right) and an Award line in the page header. Logos need a clear background and a version drawn for black (`logoOnDark`). A build that should carry the logo holds its own copy.
- Stellar Narratives' build (`public/builds/stellar-narratives/`) has no additions outside its source: the 3D view (`src/desktop/`), the shared scene template (`src/scene.js`) and the scanner shader rebuilt from the retired holographic-shaders module are in the `stellar-narratives` project folder. It is A-Frame, not the Studio runtime: stock A-Frame 1.3.0 in 3D, 8frame plus the engine only for ?view=ar, because 8frame holds the scene until XR8 loads.
- 13 Moon Calendar's build (`public/builds/13-moon-calendar/`) has no additions outside its source, the `dibiki-giizis-selfhosted` project folder. Same A-Frame setup as Stellar Narratives. It is a floor piece: the image target lies on the ground, so in the 3D view the `#ground` entity is rotated -90 on x and the camera walks around it. `src/components/moons.js` is shared with Stellar Narratives: change the moon rules in both.
- Wildlife Portals XR's build (`public/builds/wildlife-portals-xr/`) has no additions outside its source, the `wildlife-ar-selfhosted` project folder. Same A-Frame setup as Stellar Narratives. Its export had no scene code: the hosted app's bundle and assets are saved in `wildlife-ar-hosted-recovery.zip` beside that folder, the only copy left after February 28, 2027. Models are meshopt-compressed with the decoder in the build's `external/vendor/`.
- The Erratics' build (`public/builds/the-erratics/`) has no additions outside its source, the `the-erratics-selfhosted` project folder, which merges the two 8th Wall Studio exports (Central Branch, three walls, and Sherwood) into one scene. Studio runtime like Biidaaban. Its 3D view hides the runtime's depth-only hider mesh and draws a copy of each hider box painted with the mural photo, so the doorway sits in the wall. In AR all four targets track at once and the wall the camera finds loads its space. The 3D camera frames the doorway models (file names with "doorway") instead of the whole wall; their size against the mural stays as in AR. It is `hidden: true` until its models run smoothly on phones.
- `hidden: true` in project.js keeps a work off the home grid, the filters and the floating background (`projects` in registry.js). Its page still opens at `/xr/<slug>` through `getProject`, for testing on the live site.
- QR codes open the AR view directly, never the work page: `arUrl()` in `src/projects/launch.js` adds `?view=ar` to builds, so every build must open the camera AR on `?view=ar`. The code is drawn as beadwork in `src/components/BeadQR.jsx` (one bead per module, a white-bead quiet zone, hide outside it, colours from the `--bead-*` and `--hide*` tokens). After changing the bead look, decode every work's code at small sizes before pushing; the dark and light beads must keep their contrast.
- The cursor matches the portfolio (noodinstudio.com): a black ring with four tapered stems, saffron centre over anything clickable. Both are data-URI SVGs in tokens.css (`--cursor`, `--cursor-link`), the one place colours are written as hex outside a token, because a data URI cannot read custom properties. Set `cursor` only through those two tokens, never `pointer`. Builds in `public/builds/` run in their own iframe and keep the system cursor, except Aabijijiwan, which carries a copy of the cursor and the tokens in its own `style.css`.
- Type follows the portfolio's stylesheet (its `css/site.css`): same tokens, weights, leading and hover states. When a shared rule changes on one site, change it on the other. The 3D field, the card lift and the stretched landing title stay XR's own.
- `categories` in project.js says how a work is experienced: `image-target`, `world-tracking`, `3d`, any number of them. They are the coloured tags on the home cards (CategoryTags.jsx) and the first row of filters; a filter shows only when a listed work uses it. To add a category, add it to `CATEGORIES` in schema.js with a `tone`, and add the `--tag-<tone>` token plus its `.tag--` and `.filter--` rules if the tone is new. Tag text is white, so a new tag colour needs 4.5:1 against white. `kind` stays the medium.
- Aabijijiwan's build (`public/builds/aabijijiwan/`) is a plain three.js viewer of the sculpture's design model, not an 8th Wall build and not AR: the work hangs from a ceiling slab, a 1.7 m figure stands under it, and the camera is a visitor at eye height who drags to look and taps the floor to walk. Its `main.js` sets the installed size from Quinn's figures: `FOOTPRINT` (8 m long, 8 m tall, 2 m wide; the model is fitted to it per axis, since the Rev E export is 7.72 x 8.03 x 2.04) and `CLEARANCE` (lowest point 5.0 m above the floor). Its `style.css` copies the site's tokens and cursor; change them in both places. A work whose categories are only `3d` has no AR view: `hasAR()` in launch.js keeps `?view=ar` off its QR code and changes the QR label. It is `hidden: true` until the statement reviews listed in public/builds/README.md are done.
