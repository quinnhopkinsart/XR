# Notes for Claude

- Each XR work is one folder in `src/projects/<slug>/` with a `project.js` default export and a `content.json` beside it. The registry auto-discovers and merges them. Do not add works to a central list.
- `content.json` holds the text, `status` and `hidden` (`CONTENT_FIELDS` in schema.js); `project.js` holds images, launch, targets, categories, award and models. Never set a `CONTENT_FIELDS` field in project.js: `npm run check` fails on it. Quinn edits content.json through the dashboard at `/admin` (`src/admin/`, guide in `docs/THE-DASHBOARD.md`), which commits straight to `main` through the GitHub API with his token. So `main` can move between your pull and your push: fetch before editing any content.json, and keep the dashboard's form (`toForm` and `fromForm` in Admin.jsx) in step when a content field is added. The home intro is `src/brand/copy.json`. Where these notes say "in project.js" for `hidden`, text or status, read content.json.
- Field rules live in `src/projects/schema.js`; the human guide is `docs/ADDING-A-PROJECT.md`. Keep the two in sync when a field changes.
- Scaffold new works with `npm run new -- "Title"` (add `--build` for a self-hosted 8th Wall build).
- Colours and type come only from `src/brand/tokens.css`. Components never hard-code colours.
- Copy follows Quinn's voice: first person for site copy, declarative present tense for work descriptions, no em-dashes, no rhetorical questions, no "immersive", "stunning", "vibrant", "unique", "bridging two worlds". Anishinaabemowin is translated in the same clause on first use, never italicized. Non-human figures are relatives, presences and forces, never symbols.
- Run `npm run build` before committing; it runs `npm run check` first.
- 8th.io links stop working on February 28, 2027. Use `legacy` on any work still pointing at one.
- The floating background (`src/field/`) collects `models` from every project.js. Add 3D pieces there, never in the field code. The field is curated by Quinn, not every work with models shows them: right now only Trading Post Humanism, 13 Moon Calendar, Woodland Be[AR], Aabijijiwan and Biidaaban do, and Stellar Narratives, Wildlife Portals XR and The Erratics keep their .glb files with `models` commented out. Ask before adding a work's pieces to the field. Quinn wants recognition: each model is one sculpture, large and whole on screen. A `split` model is almost put together, just a little exploded (the `LOOSE` values in field.js; keep them small), and `turn` in degrees faces it to the page; an unsplit model floats whole and tumbles, except a `hero`, which faces the page. `place()` fits each sculpture to the screen by its body (the spread of its parts with the outermost few left out), so a stray orb cannot shrink the figure, and spaces them about one per screen. All sit near the focal plane (z = 0). `foreground` on a split model lifts named meshes out (Trading Post Humanism's barbed wire and its spirit lines, which are the Shaman's blue loops, SP_RingA, B and C; the red branching line in AR_GlowPaper is a thunderbird, not a spirit line) and hangs them close to the camera, far larger than the screen and running off it. On desktop a lens pass (three's BokehPass) softens that foreground, so the canvas paints the ground colour itself there; cut-out foreground planes sit out its depth pass (`flatFront`). Phones skip the lens. Keep each .glb under about 2 MB (Draco via `npx @gltf-transform/cli draco`). 13 Moon Calendar's piece is the whole work as it stands once opened (stone turtle, shell, moon, ring of thirteen moons, ring of phases) without its image target: `calendar.glb`, put together from the build's assets by `scripts/build-13-moon-field.mjs` (the build's models rest closed, so the script sets each to the end of its opening clip). Aabijijiwan's piece is a lighter copy of the viewer's model with less metallic mirrors, since the field has nothing for a mirror to reflect. Biidaaban's piece is its middle panel's 3D scene only (the rock of the mine, the neutrino detector over its circle and the spiral of particles; no panel picture, and no `skylodge`, the dark half cylinder that backs the scene inside the panel, which Quinn had removed because it covered the rest): `mine.glb`, made from the build's PANEL-2-v1.glb by `scripts/build-biidaaban-field.mjs`, which sets the scene to one moment (58 s) of its long narrated clip; `spin: ['neutrino']` keeps the spiral turning (`spin` on a split model turns named flat parts in their own plane, `SPIN` in field.js). Split sculptures keep up to 64 parts on phones and 120 elsewhere (`maxParts`): a lower cap on phones dropped the small pieces of Woodland Be[AR] and the Shaman, which Quinn noticed. 13 Moon Calendar and Biidaaban carry a `scale` above 1, because their fit to the page read small. The page's column grows with the number of sculptures (`BAND` and `PER` in field.js), so a new piece does not crowd the rest.

- The landing page has a free camera (`src/field/FlyMode.jsx`, rendered by Home.jsx only; Quinn wants it on no other page). Three ways in: the "Fly through the works" button under the intro (`FlyButton`) and the same button at the foot of the page, and scrolling on past the end of the page, which fills the bar on the foot's button (the `CHARGE` values; `rest` keeps the tail of a fast trackpad scroll from opening it by accident). The button is the way in for keyboards and for reduced-motion visitors, who get no scroll trigger, so never remove it. Inside, `html.is-flying` fades the page out, the page is `inert`, and field.js hands the camera over (`fly`, `flyForward` and `flyHome` in motion.js; the `FLY` values and `flyStep` in field.js): drag to look (same direction as the Aabijijiwan viewer), wheel, W A S D, Q and E or the arrow keys to move, a joystick on touch screens in free roam only, hidden on the tour (`Joystick` in FlyMode.jsx, `flyStick` in motion.js; Quinn chose it over a hold-down button), a Tilt button on phones that lets tilting the phone look around in flight (`TILT_LOOK` in field.js, negative because the first version looked the wrong way on Quinn's phone; the same `tilt` as the page's Tilt button) and a hold-down Fly button on other screens, Reset view, and Back to the page or Esc to leave, after which the camera eases back. The lens stays on in flight on desktop, at Quinn's request for depth of field: it focuses on the work nearest the middle of the view and softens the rest (`LENS` in field.js, a wider tolerance than on the page because distances are longer). A "Depth of field" button in the flight's controls turns it off and on there (`flyLens` in motion.js, remembered between visits; shown only where the lens exists, `hasLens`). The page itself always keeps the lens. The layout works from `CAM_Z`, never from the camera's own position, so flying does not move the sculptures. The loop runs in flight even when Pause is on or motion is reduced, with the sculptures held still. In flight the field opens out (`flyMix` in field.js, 0 on the page, 1 in flight): each work's sculptures leave the page's column and gather in that work's own zone, side by side and facing the centre, with the work's title under them, the widest zone at eye level straight ahead and the rest on an arc (`ZONES` and `planZones`; a zone has no floor, Quinn does not want one). `flight: { scale, lift }` on a model makes it larger and higher in flight than on the page: Aabijijiwan hangs high and three times its page size, large and impressive overhead, at Quinn's request. A negative `lift` sinks a zone below the camera, with its title over it: Biidaaban sits well down, at twice its page size, because its detector is in a mine (Quinn's request). Zones that hang high or sit low go to the sides of the arc. `flight.backdrop: [fill, outline]` on one of a work's models stands a colour field close behind that work's zone: Trading Post Humanism has one, pink (`--field-pink`) with a saffron outline, at Quinn's request. `src/field/scenery.js` adds what stands far off, built the first time someone flies and seen only in flight, where the haze draws back (`HAZE`): large colour fields (round but uneven, organic, each with a thick outline of one even width in a second colour: `blob`, `FIELD_COLOURS`, `FIELD_LINE`), four-pointed stars, + and * marks, and stick pine trees (the `SCENERY` values). One dashed black line runs through it for the visitor to follow (`TRAIL` in scenery.js): a sine wave that loops past every work in turn, up to the high ones and down to the low ones, with its waves running slowly along it (still when motion is paused or reduced). The line joins one viewpoint per work, a place that frames the whole work (from below for one that hangs high, from above for one sunk low), and runs a little under them, with its corners rounded off (`smooth`). The flight opens as a tour along it (`flyTour` in motion.js; Quinn chose the tour as the default, with free flight behind the "Free roam" button), starting at the work straight ahead: the wheel, a swipe on touch screens, the keys or the joystick carry the camera from viewpoint to viewpoint, always looking at a work and turning steadily from one to the next between viewpoints, never along the line (Quinn found the first version's angles jumped about; `tour.poseAt` in scenery.js, `TOUR`, `setTour` and `tourStep` in field.js); a mouse drag looks aside and drifts back. "Free roam" hands the camera over for free flight; pressed again, it returns to the line from the nearest place on it. Behind all the works runs one large spirit line (`SPIRIT` in scenery.js, colour `--spirit-line`, the light blue of the Shaman's spirit lines): a thick line that loops round the outside of the ring of works and meanders, with rimmed orbs joined to it by short stems. Two sine waves move along it, one up and down and one in and out, so it snakes, and its thickness swells in a gentle pulse, all done in its shader from how far along the line each point is; the orbs ride the waves and breathe with it. Quinn asked for a subtle pulse, then for more sine wave motion and more meander than the first version had, so the waves are plain to see and the pulse stays gentle. The pines are flat, black and really simple, a few lines like a drawing, kept further out than the camera can fly and always turned flat on to it; keep them that way. All of it is flat colour in the palette's tokens.
