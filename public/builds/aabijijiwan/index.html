Aabijijiwan

CNC-cut plywood, acrylic mirror, steel, steel cable. Suspended in the atrium of the John Innes Community Recreation Centre, Moss Park, Tkaronto.

Aabijijiwan is Anishinaabemowin for "it flows continuously." The sculpture is one river shown at five moments in its life, hung as five layers of CNC-cut wood. The youngest moment hangs highest, its channel almost straight. By the lowest layer the bends have grown until the river is two-thirds longer than where it began. The five channels run parallel through a shared space and never touch, the way the two rows do on Kaswentha, the Two Row Wampum belt. Set into each one is a line of mirror tiles, like the beads of the belt, clear on one layer and purple on the next.

Design model, Rev E. A City of Toronto public art commission. The atrium is rebuilt from the architects' diagrams and is approximate. The figure is 1.7 m tall.
